What Determines Your Scrap Car’s Value

Vehicle Weight and Price Per Tonne Model

Scrap dealers pay for your car based on its weight, not its age. Heavier vehicles contain more steel, so they earn more money. Dealers use a weighbridge to measure your car’s exact weight. Many people get confused between price per tonne and price per hundredweight. A tonne equals 1000 kilograms, while hundredweight is a much smaller unit. Always ask your dealer which measurement they use before accepting any quote.

Scrap Steel Index and Why Prices Change Weekly

Scrap car prices move up and down every week. This happens because steel prices follow London Metal Exchange and industry data from British Metals Recycling Association. Global supply and demand push ferrous scrap prices higher or lower. Currency exchange rates also affect how much UK dealers can pay. No single dealer controls these prices alone. The whole market shifts together based on these outside forces.

Make, Model, Age and Condition as Value Modifiers

Your car’s make and model can still matter, even for scrap. Some models have parts that are in higher demand than others. Mileage and overall condition also affect how much you receive. A car with a clean engine is worth more than a rusted one. Bodywork damage and corrosion often lower final offer. Dealers check all of this before giving you a number.

Whether Car Is Complete, Drivable or Missing Parts

A complete vehicle always earns more than one missing parts. If your engine, wheels, or catalytic converter are gone, expect a lower quote. Dealers deduct value for every missing component they find. A drivable car is easier and cheaper to collect than one that cannot move. Non drivable cars may need special towing equipment, which can reduce your offer. Keeping your car intact is simplest way to protect its value.

Your Location and Why Quotes Vary by Postcode

Where you live changes how much your scrap car is worth. Areas with more Authorised Treatment Facilities often offer slightly better prices. This happens because of lower transport and collection costs nearby. Rural locations sometimes see smaller offers due to longer travel distances. Urban areas with dense dealer networks tend to have more competition. This competition can actually work in your favour when comparing quotes.

Average UK Scrap Car Prices in 2026

Price Range by Vehicle Weight

Small cars typically weigh less and earn a smaller scrap payout. Family cars and SUVs weigh more, so they usually fetch higher prices. Vans and 4x4 vehicles often sit at top of weight bracket. Heavier vehicles generally mean more steel and more money for you. Prices shift slightly depending on current market rates each week. Always check updated figures before accepting any offer.

Sample £/Tonne Benchmarks and How to Read Them

Benchmark prices give you a helpful starting point for comparison. These are sample rates, not guaranteed figures for your exact car. Use them as a reference when checking if a quote seems fair. If an offer sits far below benchmark, ask dealer why. Transparency matters, and a good dealer will explain their pricing clearly. This helps you avoid confusion and feel confident in your decision.

Why No Legitimate Dealer Can Quote a Fixed Price Per Tonne

Honest dealers cannot promise one fixed price for every car. Scrap metal prices change too often to stay same. Any dealer offering a guaranteed fixed rate may not be trustworthy. Legitimate businesses explain that prices depend on current market. This honesty protects you from unrealistic expectations later. A provisional price is normal and shows dealer is being fair.

What Individual Car Parts Are Worth in Scrap vs Resale

Scrap Car Battery Value

Car batteries hold real scrap value, even in an old vehicle. Lead acid batteries are common and easy to recycle safely. On average, a scrap battery earns around five pounds. This amount can shift slightly depending on battery recycling demand. Weight also plays a small role in final battery price. It is a small but steady part of your total payout.

Radiator Value

Radiators vary in value depending on what they are made from. Older radiators often contain copper or brass, which are worth more. Modern radiators are usually made from aluminium instead. Copper and brass radiators fetch higher prices than aluminium ones. The size and weight of your radiator also affects its scrap price. Checking your radiator type can help you estimate its value.

Catalytic Converter and Alternator Value

Catalytic converters contain precious metals like platinum and palladium. This makes them one of most valuable parts in your car. Alternators and starter motors also hold decent resale value separately. Selling these parts individually can sometimes earn more than scrapping the whole car. However, catalytic converters are often targeted by thieves, so handle with care. Always use a licensed buyer when selling these valuable parts.

Tyres, Alloys and Mechanical Components

Good tyre tread can add real value to your scrap car. Alloy wheels usually sell for more than basic steel wheels. Mechanical parts like gearboxes and transmissions can also be sold separately. Turbos and injectors often have strong demand in resale market. Suspension components and brake rotors hold smaller but steady value. These small parts add up when sold individually.

Should You Strip Parts First or Sell Car Whole?

Parting out your car can earn more money in total. However, this process takes real time and storage space to manage. Selling individual parts on platforms like eBay requires patience and effort. A whole car sale is faster and much less work for you. Intact vehicles also earn a premium compared to stripped ones. Weigh time cost against extra money before deciding.

Keeping Car Complete vs Removing Parts First

Removing parts before scrapping often reduces your final payout. Dealers deduct value for every missing original component they notice. A complete vehicle earns a clear premium over a stripped one. Even small missing items can lower your overall offer. It is usually smarter to leave everything in place until collection. This keeps your negotiating position strong and your price higher.

Driving Fuel Tank Down Before Collection

Your scrap car does not need a full tank of fuel. Driving tank down before collection helps you use up fuel value. With rising fuel prices, this small step saves you money. Dealers do not pay extra for fuel left inside tank. Using it beforehand means nothing goes to waste. This simple habit adds a little extra value to your decision.

Presenting Vehicle

Accurate details prevent surprises or price changes on collection day. Keep your documentation ready and easy to find. Good presentation can genuinely support a smoother, fairer process.

Licensed vs. Unlicensed Scrap Dealers: Why It Affects Both Price and Legality

What an Authorised Treatment Facility Is

An Authorised Treatment Facility, or ATF, is a licensed recycling centre. These facilities follow strict rules set by Environment Agency. The End of Life Vehicles Regulations 2003 require proper depollution before recycling. This process safely removes fluids and hazardous materials from your car. Using an ATF ensures your vehicle is disposed of legally. It also protects you from future liability issues.

Why Unlicensed Dealers Offer Higher Quotes and Real Cost

Unlicensed traders sometimes offer tempting, higher quotes upfront. However, these deals often come with serious hidden risks. Illegal scrap dealers may fly tip parts instead of recycling them properly. This can lead to environmental fines that fall back on you. Prosecution risk increases when your car is not disposed of legally. A slightly lower, legitimate offer is always safer choice.

Red Flags of an Untrustworthy Scrap Buyer

Watch for dealers who refuse to give you a receipt. Cash only payment demands should raise immediate concern. Pressure tactics asking you to decide instantly are another warning sign. A trustworthy dealer will always offer a Certificate of Destruction. If a buyer cannot show ATF registration, walk away immediately. These simple checks protect you from scams and unnecessary risk.

Legal Paperwork You Must Complete When Scrapping a Car

Notifying DVLA and Deadline That Applies

You must notify DVLA as soon as your car is scrapped. This is done using V5C/14 section of your logbook. GOV.UK scrapping guidance explains this process in full detail. Most reputable dealers handle this notification for you directly. Still, it is wise to confirm it has been completed. This step officially ends your legal responsibility for vehicle.

Certificate of Destruction and Why You Must Keep It

A Certificate of Destruction is your legal proof car is gone. This document confirms vehicle was recycled through a licensed facility. Always ask for this certificate and keep it somewhere safe. It protects you if any questions arise about vehicle later. Without it, proving proper disposal becomes much harder. Treat this certificate as an important official record.

What Happens If You Don’t Notify DVLA

Failing to notify DVLA keeps you legally responsible for car. This means continued liability for road tax and insurance charges. You could even face a fine for not notifying them in time. This risk exists even if car has already been scrapped. Notifying DVLA promptly closes this responsibility completely. It is a simple step that avoids real financial trouble.

Cancelling and Reclaiming Road Tax

Cancelling your road tax starts refund process automatically. The DVLA issues refunds for any full months remaining on your tax. If you pay by direct debit, this is cancelled at same time. Refunds are usually sent directly without extra paperwork needed. This process is quick once DVLA has been notified. Keeping track of dates helps you confirm your refund arrives.

Cancelling Your Car Insurance

Contact your insurer as soon as your car has been scrapped. Most insurers offer a pro rata refund for unused policy months. This means you only pay for time your car was insured. Cancelling promptly prevents paying for coverage you no longer need. Keep records of your cancellation for future reference. This small step ensures you are not overpaying unnecessarily.
